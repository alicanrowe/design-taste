# Influences and references

Borrow the hand, never the product. Public text (site copy, captions, sheets) never names these references.
Each collection's own brief sets the weights; this is the shared list of who means what.

## Metal and jewelry
- **moiraxmel** (the client's own engraved-feather jewellery): carved realistic feathers that wrap the body,
  petals with spiky leaves, thorned hearts, gatepiece scrolls, big claw-set stones, fine chain and pear drops.
- **hyodomachine** (liquid-chrome tattoo calligraphy): ribbons that swell to needle points, mirrored placements.
  Use as the accent; an all-liquid piece "reads as plumbing" ("not a fan of this one").
- Baroque, Art Nouveau and Gothic (lancet arches, rose windows, interlaced tracery, thorns).
- "Pick your utensil" sculpted instrument renders (Instagram DUUOA7KDGrX): ornament density and metal finish bar.
- Harold & Saxon chisels: the bar for tool finish (mirror flats, blackened bevels, hairline lands).
- **AERCRAWLER pierced tracery signet** (aercrawler.com, via an Instagram story, 24 Nov, "add this to jewlerry skill
  and style ref"; `img/tracery_signet_aercrawler.jpg`): a chunky oval silver signet whose face is pierced through with
  Gothic quatrefoil and trefoil tracery inside a plain bezel rim, pierced shoulders carrying the ornament down the
  shank, oxidised near-black recesses against polished high points, worn with wide brushed and hairline-textured plain
  bands. What the client likely likes: real voids (pierced, not just carved), heavy signet mass, ornament held inside a
  clean rim, old worn-silver contrast, one loud piece set off by quiet textured bands.

## Leather, bags and boots
- **Japanese craft leather workshops** (from the client's saves, the most-saved makers overall): veg-tan,
  kakishibu and shell cordovan, hand saddle-stitching, solid brass, soft roomy bag shapes, colours from tan to
  oxblood, forest green and teal. See `instagram-saves.md` S1.
- **Japanese repro denim and workwear** (saves): selvedge, chain-stitch, patina, horsehide jackets, engineer
  boots. Honest construction and wear over ornament. S2.
- **Kei Kagami** AW2014 shoes: metal structure that *is* the heel (rib cage, spine), plain uppers so the metal leads.
- **Söderberg**: knife-edge restraint, one precise structural metal part, tilted block heel, black.
- **Ives Barrera**: bespoke last shapes (chisel toe, fiddle waist), welted finish, hardware as jewelry (cast floral
  buckles, beaded zip tassels), terraced heels, nailed soles.
- **gsongim**: crushed soft leather against crisp metal, knots and tethers as closures, stacked straps, ivory
  thread, poetic Korean names. The bojagi seat came from here and was loved.
- **Studio Wenjüe Lu**: washed greys and indigo, scalloped petal hems, poetic names with archive codes.
- **Komune NYC** (and what it stocks: gsongim, untitlab, XLIM, O.Files, Blackmerle, Ella Roberts): the quality and
  sellability bar.
- **14th Addiction** (Japanese 90s-2000s archive leather; 23 Nov, the client: "add these as pretty heavily weighted
  influences"). Reference piece: a 1-of-1 oxblood goatskin rider on a silk lining. Weight: HEAVY for jackets and boots.
  What to take: a real fitted rider/cafe-racer pattern (stand collar with a buckle strap, two-piece sleeves to the
  wrist, quilted elbows, sleeve zips); python-skin inlays on the collar lining, hem band and side panels; side seams
  laced with leather cord ending in tassels; a raw, torn hem band; a horizontal chest zip crossing the centre zip
  (pale zip tapes drawn as lines); a small silver plate on the back yoke; worn, waxy, finely creased leather.
  Files: boots-bags/refs/influence_1123/14th_*.jpg.
- **Y'2 Leather x IKIYA** "STEER OIL W RIDER'S COAT" (SR-55, made in Japan; same weight, same day). A long
  double-rider coat in thick oiled steerhide: big notched lapel with snap studs, epaulets, asymmetric brass zip,
  a large curved patch panel carrying a flap pocket and a zip pocket, sleeve zips, a western-point back yoke and a
  centre-back vent, jade-green contrast stitching and a jade satin lining (the studio accent #1D847E).
  What to take: heavy honest construction, contrast stitching as a design line, bright hardware on black, a coat
  length. Files: boots-bags/refs/influence_1123/y2_*.jpg.
- Both: transform, never copy. No logo plates, no copied pocket shapes or panel layouts.
  23 Nov, the client on the round 5 boots and rider: "these are a bit copy-esque bro". Taking the refs' signature
  details read as copying, even on our own silhouettes: python inlays, cord-laced side seams with tassels, jade contrast
  stitch on black, crossing chest zips, torn hem bands. So take only the HAND: worn waxy hide, heavy honest
  construction, small real cast hardware, the feeling of a 1-of-1. Every visible signature detail must come from our
  own vocabulary (halo, horn, lancet arch, thorn, corset busk, armour lame, coat flap) and be drawn our way.
- Bayonetta and Devil May Cry, aesthetic only: gothic and baroque, angelic vs demonic, dramatic heels, ornate
  silver, sweeping coat-like lines. Never a character's actual garment; never named in public.

## Wood and furniture
- **Fawn Orchard** (the client's own carved work): gouge-scalloped terrain along the grain, pierced lancet openings,
  pod hollows, walnut, oiled.
- **Gregory Beson**: the render and staging bar for furniture (soft high window light, paper sweep, linen curtain,
  limewash room, measured walnut tone).
- Krenov, the Center for Furniture Craftsmanship: joinery and proportion.
- From the client's saves (`instagram-saves.md` S3): Nakashima (live edge, butterfly keys), sculpted rockers in
  the Maloof line, Windsors, wiped-urushi Japanese cabinets, carved gouge-ripple and fluted surfaces, wood paired
  with stone or clear resin as equals.
- A stone console the client sent for the shrine bench (22 Nov, "for shrine bench maybe"): a smooth, light flow
  inlaid in the top of a dark, rough-hewn slab, its banks one long slow wave, a chiselled rim round it. Used: the
  two-finish contrast (one quiet smooth field against dark carved terrain) and the slow-wave bank. Not used: its
  pour-to-the-floor leg, which is a drip (banned).

- **metastudio "gravity table"** (Istanbul, reel DfxACqzjqV3, 22 Nov, "i really like the look of this reel"): a
  round clear glass top on two glass sheets slotted into a cross, with a dye cloud inside the glass that pools
  under the top and pulls down the cross to the floor like a funnel or a drop of ink falling (brown, blue,
  burgundy, black). Used: colour that lives *inside* a clear material as a gradient, the structure itself
  carrying the motif (the funnel is the base), the plain slotted cross, one colourway per piece. Not used as
  is: plain smooth glass alone would fail A5; for us the gradient goes in cast clear resin or glass that also
  carries carved or cast relief.

- **medium things "light doors"** (reel Dd_JOR3sZgR, 22 Nov, "cool too"): a slim wall light built from stacked
  3D-printed fluted panels in offset bands. The ribs act as lenses, so each band blends from dark to glowing
  as the viewing angle changes, and turning the lamp makes the bands flip light and dark against each other.
  Shown in one handheld mirror shot in a dark bathroom, the lamp as the only light. Used: light that changes
  with the viewer's angle, the motif built into the diffuser's own surface (ribs, not a printed pattern),
  banded blocks offset like a shifted stack. For lamps (VIGIL), shoot it turning in the dark so the effect
  is the subject.

## Furniture: restraint (lead references for seating, 23 Nov)
The client, after the cast chairs read as kitsch: "maybe look at tom sachs, frank gehry, and dieter rams chair
design principles". Take the principles, never their products (no Wiggle, no 620, no Sachs plywood replicas).
- **Dieter Rams** (Braun, Vitsoe 620 chair programme): less, but better. As little design as possible; honest,
  unobtrusive, long-lasting, thorough down to the last detail. Use: one idea, every part earning its place,
  edges, feet and fixings designed as carefully as the main form. A quiet piece still has to be finished.
- **Frank Gehry** (Easy Edges cardboard furniture, the bent-maple Cross Check chairs): the material makes the
  gesture. Laminated cardboard shown edge-on, thin wood strips bent and woven so they hold each other with no
  hardware. Use: one gesture that only this material and process can make (a fold, a pour, a lamination, a
  bend), the section or edge left visible so you can read how it was made.
- **Tom Sachs** (studio furniture and sculpture from plywood, foam core, steel and stock hardware; "Ten Bullets",
  "always be knolling"): honest, visible fabrication. Standard stock, exposed screws and bolts, seams and marks
  left in, nothing faked. Use: fasteners, tie holes, parting seams and cut edges as the only ornament; a part
  list a shop could buy from.
- Together, for cast seating: one cast mass and as few other parts as possible, joined with fixings you can see
  and count, an idea that comes from the cast or the bend, mood from proportion and colour (principles A13).

- **breados.doc, "thinking about fun things to do with steel"** (Instagram DfyBcRmiGKG, 23 Nov, sent by the
  client as the inspo after "i hate them all too boring"): a bookshelf as a stage set. Laser-cut brushed stainless
  plate in a big, asymmetric, layered thicket (pine bough, willow strands, broad leaves) rising off a stainless
  base plate; small open steel boxes of books perched at four heights inside it; every joint is lab hardware you can
  read: round-bar posts with split clamp collars, turnbuckle tie rods, bolted gussets, round foot discs, pan-head
  screws. Use: function set *inside* a sculpture, not the sculpture shaped like the function; one material in one
  finish; density and overlap (many plates at different depths) instead of one plane; asymmetry and reach; the
  hardware treated as jewelry (collars, turnbuckles, foot discs); fun. Watch: the plant outline is depicted (A13,
  A5 flat cut-out); here it works because it is openly a cut plate held up by a visible rig, like a theatre flat,
  and because the precise hardware wins over the outline. Don't copy the plants, the shelf boxes or the layout.

  gloves, black clothes and white or grey, so the piece is the only colour.

## Presentation and grade (liked reel looks)
- **"Specimen sheet" grade** (from the gravity table reel): a pale warm-grey seamless with film grain, the object
  centred small and floating in corner crop marks, a tiny monospace caption ("gravity table · brown") and
  coordinate readouts, then real-room shots with white tracking boxes and hairlines pinned to parts of the piece,
  a saturated label chip (ultramarine block, white mono text), and an end card in flat ultramarine with a small
  chrome logo. Real light: one hard window light with a sharp floor shadow in a grey concrete room. Cuts on the
  beat through each colourway. Use for turntables, colourway reels and site sheets; keep the client's brand fonts and
  white backgrounds (D-rule) for stills unless the reel is the point.

- **Preferred reel format: reference to result** (Impermanence Studios reel DgDhn3HgLt9, 22 Nov: "reels like this
  that show how the inspo became a new thing is super cool"). One locked-off shot, no cuts, about 14 s. The
  finished piece sits on a plinth against a dark block wall in hard side light, with a short white caption
  over it ("this is my art"). From 0.5 s, reference photos (archival shoes, armour, beaded corsets) drop onto
  the frame one at a time, about one every 0.25 s, overlapping into a collage that covers the piece. Under the
  collage a hand pulls a wrapper off the piece. From about 7 s the references fall away in reverse, and the
  bare finished piece holds clean for the last 3 s with the caption back on. How to make ours: pair each
  finished piece with 8 to 15 of the references it really came from (the logged reels, the saves, the
  influences on this page), stack them over a still or turntable of the piece, peel them away, then hold the
  result. Use real sources only, never invented ones; references are shown, not named in captions (see the top
  of this page). For a longer cut, put a beat between groups, one per motif: "these thorns became this claw".

- **"Neo-tribal" column pull** (tddsash reel Dfv_wF4K-WD, 23 Nov, "can this be used for one of our reels
  somewhere"): 18 s, 1:1, a generative glyph of overlapping compass arcs welded at pinched nodes, in one teal on
  pale ground. The motion is a column pull: the frame splits into vertical strips of uneven width; in each strip
  the image shows below a cut row and that row is stretched upward and fades, so the glyph seems drawn up out of
  streaks, strip by strip. Thin construction circles and hollow boxes sit over it. Hard cuts on the beat
  (about every 1 to 2.5 s) reshuffle the composition. Worth taking: the strip pull as a reveal or transition, and
  construction lines showing under the finished form. Not to copy: the arc-and-node glyph, the teal duotone, the
  decorative circles. Ours (Flatwork reel "the pull", reels/flatwork-pull/pull.py): the piece is pulled up out of
  its own real DXF cut sheet in strips; the cut row is an IKIYA-green hairline with a hollow square node (the
  studio measuring mark); the same pull whips to the next piece. Real cut files only, never decorative geometry.

- **"Volume X-ray" render** (Jess Wiseman reel Dd4amcZBKQm, 23 Nov, "this is super cool can we use it
  somewhere?"): a hard-surface product (a pod-shaped case with a ring pull) shown as a glowing see-through scan,
  every inner wall, boss and channel visible at once as grainy light. How it's made (caption: "model, volume,
  solidify"): Blender Cycles; the mesh gets Mirror + Subdivision, then Solidify at about -0.02 m (Simple, Fill
  rim on) so every surface becomes a thin shell; the material is a Volume Scatter (white, density about 11,
  anisotropy 0) with no surface shader, so light builds up where you look through the most shell: edges and
  inner walls glow and flat faces go faint. 500 samples, Blackman-Harris filter, rendered on black. Graded in
  After Effects: monochrome tint, a Screen-mode halation layer for glow, stacked adjustment layers; the end
  frames add thin mono UI type and a target ring. Worth taking: the construction is the image (hidden joints,
  magnets, inner walls read in one frame), and the grainy scatter reads as a scan or specimen, not CG gloss.
  Not to copy: the cyan-on-black gadget palette and the pod object. Ours: show hidden construction in our own
  pieces, graded to the studio look (white ground, IKIYA green tags), e.g. as a radiograph negative (dark
  scatter on white). Build note (first test, Nave tray): on hard plate CAD, a Principled Volume with emission
  only (density 0) on 0.4 mm solidified shells, black world, then inverted, reads cleaner than Volume Scatter,
  which saturated or washed out; add the grain and halation in the finish.

## Studio look for reels (23 Nov, the client: "all the reels we post ... feel like they're from the same design studio")
Every reel finishes through the shared studio finishing kit (studio.py, STUDIO_LOOK.md): one key-light pool with
falloff, neutral-warm palette, the same grade, brand type top-left with a mono counter, hairline crop marks, hairline
tracking boxes, one continuous camera. Revised the same night: labels sit on solid accent tags (title, spec,
counter) with a hairline leader to the piece, and the end card is the accent; accent is IKIYA lining green #1D847E
("electric blue doesn't necessarily fit the aesthetic of what we make"). Plain type with no tag read generic and
hard to read.
