# Principles: what the client likes and hates

Each rule carries the client's words and a strike count (how many separate times it has come up). Higher counts
are the ones that keep getting missed: check them hardest. Newest critique wins over older wording.

## A. The core of the taste (read these even if you read nothing else)

**A1. Realised, not simple. (6 strikes)** Every round that read as a plain base object with an idea attached was
rejected: boots r1 "these designs all suck and feel unrealised", boots r3 "how simple they are", the Swarm moths
"these three motifs seem unrealised", the jewelry "a bit samey", the Bombyx lotus ring "this feels kinda basic and boring". A design is realised when its idea runs through
the whole object, every member is modelled, and it would hold up next to the best maker in its field.

**A2. The motif is the structure, never a thing stuck on. (6 strikes)** What gets loved: the heel that *is* the
hoop, the foot that is the leg continuing (Durumi's crane foot: "does the best job at being consistent with the
theme"), wings whose longest feather lands on the leg as a brace, a jail grille that is the cast lancet with the
moth hanging from it ("fucking sick"). What gets rejected: a ram horn ring bolted on an ankle ("so stupid and
gimmicky"), a ball on a stalk to pass a tip rule ("this ball is weird", "lumpy spike"), a small floating ring
behind a heel, a bug pictogram pinned at a crossing ("the literal bug sucks"), straight clip bars poking out of a
body. Test: delete the feature in your head. If what is left is a normal, plain object, the feature was a gimmick.

**A3. One language, head to toe. (5 strikes)** Every part speaks the piece's own language: "this part of carved
doesnt fit the rest of the piece" (smooth chrome hooks on an all-carved piece), "the smooth abdomen ... doesn't
fit", "why do they all have elf shoes" (one stock foot on every variant), "this handle is awful, have you learned
nothing" (a generic handle on a gothic case). New parts are built from motifs already on the piece; list them
before designing. Hero views hide the odd one out: look at a close-up of every part.

**A4. Consistent detail quality. (5 strikes)** "the details are inconsistent in quality", "crazy low poly",
"line weights seem unresolved", "like 2% too messy", "why cant these petals be as detailed as those feathers?"
(Bombyx: the carved LOULAN feathers were "a really cool piece"; the open-outline petals beside them were not). The
best-detailed piece in a set is the bar for every other piece in it. Every part is modelled to the same fidelity and finish. A
richly cast buckle on a default-shell boot, lames on the sleeve of an otherwise blank jacket, or a stitched line
where its neighbours are cast relief all read as unfinished.

**A5. Sculpted surfaces, no flat dead fields. (3 strikes)** "they have a lot of flat surfaces", "the plain band
side reads weak; every face of a ring should carry detail" ("meh from this view"), flat laser-cut plates
"crude" twice ("i dont like try again", "this sucks") until the same design was cast in relief. Surfaces are
modelled: layered in depth, relief, overlap, over-and-under, crimping, moulding, carving. Quiet areas exist, but
they are deliberate rest fields bounded by detail (A9), not big undifferentiated panels.

**A6. Solid, never flimsy or floppy. (9 strikes)** "details that are flimsy or floppy", "this spike seems
fragile", "seems fragile" (earring wire), "these gaps would be too weak in real life", "not possible in real
life" (long thin dangling tail), "cant have any floating pieces bro", "is this not too fragile could it support
the weight of a man", "wavy as hell" (bars), "this being the only connection point seems a lil weak" (a stone head carried on one vine
tip). A head or any heavy part is tied in at several points and is seen to be: members flow into it, wrap under it
or root into a seat, never end as a stub touching its side. Details read heavy and stiff: thick roots, tied-in ends, real
section, closed loops. Nothing dangles, droops, flaps, wobbles or floats. Hanging parts are short and
deliberate (the unequal chain drops are the exception that was loved: short, cast as one, weighty stones).

**A7. Dark, gothic, menacing beats pretty. (4 strikes)** "a lot scarier or gothic", "have you learned nothing",
the jail moth hanging like a bat, thorned vines ("love the spiked vines running up the posts"), oxidised metal,
oxblood leather ("perfect just make the leather red"), angelic vs demonic, baroque. Tidy, cute, generic or
decorative-for-its-own-sake ornament fails even when clean.

**A8. Creative and original, never generic or classic. (3 strikes)** Boots r2: "super ugly and unoriginal ...
your understanding of design is somehow getting worse. I want them to be more creative." Benchmarking against
classic types produced classic-looking work. Take function and makeability from classics; take the look from the
brief. Every design needs one silhouette idea you can see in the main view.

**A9. One focal point and quiet space. (6 strikes)** "this is kinda busy", "too much going on" (twice), "messy",
"noisy and random", "a mess" (a whole stud family), "this a bit of a mess geometrically" (Bombyx streamers that
crossed and merged at random). Three or four motifs per piece, detail clustered at joins and
terminals, a focal point with breathing room, crossing members passing clean. Busy is not the same as realised:
A1 is about every part being designed, A9 about where the eye lands.

**A10. Sellable at the best store in its field. (standing bar)** "the product should be sellable at Commune ...
run recursive checking until you think this is a product they would make stock". Artisanal, considered, made of
real materials by a real process, hardware as jewelry. Not costume, not cosplay, not a render trick.

## B. Line, form and finish

**B1. Strokes swell and taper; curves accelerate. (4)** Every element is one continuous stroke: start, swell,
needle end. Tight at the head, long sweep at the tail. No even-gauge tubes ("even tube thickness" is the most
common robotic flag), no kinks ("maybe smooth this curve", "needs smoothing", "geomtry of this curve is weird").

**B2. Smooth, fair tapers; no lumps or blobs. (4)** "fix this being lumpy", "lumpy tumour", "body is mega
fucked", "body is weird". Long spikes and horns taper smoothly; junctions blend without bulges.

**B3. No nubs, no ball ends, no stubs. (10)** "ewwww nub", "bum ass nub", "this nub on the tail is awful",
"nice curve ruined by nub", "this ball is weird", "these nubs could be smoothed so it makes more of a circle shape",
"think these points should be sharper" (Bombyx streamer tips ending in rounded nubs). Every end tapers to a clean point, curls, or ends blunt in its
own section.

**B4. Spikes are sharp and grow from the member. (3)** "make this spike sharp and smoothly go into the rest of
the piece as its one piece of metal". Spikes wind along long runs ("spiked vines running up the posts"), never a
straight comb ("these are ugly tho"). Wearables round off points ("prolly too sharp").

**B5. Taut where it should be taut. (2)** "wavy as hell", "eww wavy as hell": bars, frames and straps stay
straight and tensioned. Melting or drooping forms are out ("too dali esque", "awful").

**B6. Real-world fidelity in the render. (4)** "crazy low poly", "is the metal clipping through the glass that
isnt possible", leather that renders like rubber, milky stones. Render like a product photo: dense geometry, real
bevels, matte calf, bright silver with dark recesses, correctly coloured stones. "dont clip them", "always run a
clipping check for everything always man".

**B8. A contour that reads as one curve is one curve. (1)** "these nubs could be smoothed so it makes more of a
circle shape" (the inner edge of a crescent kinked where it met the horns). When two members together draw an
arc, circle or line, build that contour as one curve and hang the members off it, so the eye never hits a knuckle.

**B9. Ordered rhythm in bundles. (1)** "this a bit of a mess geometrically" (Bombyx ribbons). Strands, ribbons
and vines in a group share one rule: an even fan, a common wave, graded lengths, stacked in depth. They don't
cross at random or merge into each other; a crossing is a single deliberate over-and-under.

**B7. Asymmetry is bold or absent. (3)** "aysmetrical details should be bigger and more obvious so they seem more
purposeful", "asymetrical?" on a jittery wing. Mirror the pair, or make one element clearly, intentionally
different.

## C. Motif and meaning

**C1. Never literal. (5)** "this butterfly motif is far too literal", "the literal bug sucks", "without the
butterfly motif this is kinda cool", "feather antennas are still weird" (feathers used as antennae, 5 strikes).
Creatures are suggested by structure (veins, bones, wing membranes that brace), may be partial or abstract, and
are never clip-art or stencils.

**C2. Motifs read at viewing distance. (3)** Small motifs that can't carry their detail get scaled up into real
members or dropped. Stitch-only lines vanish on a garment; hardware under about 3 mm reads as nothing.

**C3. No borrowed brand signatures. (2)** "a chrome hearts rip off isnt what i am looking for". Crosses,
daggers, fleur-de-lis terminals are out. Never copy a game character's actual garment or a maker's product.

**C4. Each piece has roots and a story. (1)** Collections get researched history, named pieces, and every motif
has a reason. Names are single poetic nouns with an archive code.

## D. Colour and stones

**D1. Light stones, one species per piece. (7)** Deep garnet "a lil dark for my taste", "stones a bit dark",
deep London blue "dont like this stone color" (x3). Use the light end: aquamarine, sky or swiss blue, rhodolite,
malaya, morganite, pale amethyst; warm citrine as a single accent is welcome ("interesting stone choice i like
it"). Same stone on both of a pair. Stones sit on a clear anchor ("odd placement of stone").

**D2. Palette.** Black, washed charcoal, washed indigo, oxblood red, natural tan; bright or oxidised silver.
Leather in black and red when asked for colourways.

## E. How the client works with a design (process taste)

**E1. When they like it, refine; don't redesign.** "i do really like the designs tho so if we dont have to change
them thatd be ideal"; the tools "based on the old ones ... improvements in the geometry and increasing the
complexity". Keep silhouette, proportion and materials; add real-world detail.

**E2. A liked change goes everywhere.** "i love these new feet for thread why arent they everywhere keep the
updates eveyrwhere its a rule". Adapt it to each piece's motif. Named exceptions stay (Durumi's own feet).

**E3. A runner-up isn't dead.** "2 is cool but i really like 1 too so keep that in mind when u do the side panels
and jail". Carry the liked option's motif onto the next surfaces of the same piece.

**E4. When the client loves a cast piece, everything that borrows its motif is cast too.** Flat versions were
rejected twice; the cast one was "fucking sick".

**E5. Match the reference until it is one to one.** "train more on the images", "til the CAD looks 1 to 1 to the
images", "recursively improve it til it can produce renders that match syd". Render loops measure against the
reference (colour, light, camera) rather than eyeballing.

**E6. Do what was asked, no more; and be autonomous.** When the client says "no redesign", don't. When they say
"I don't want to check anything", make every call, publish the passing set and report once.

**E7. Physically real.** "recursively check for real world fabricability", "keep in mind what leather i will buy
... so it can be actually produced", "would these wires be purchased?". Every part names its material, stock size
and process.
