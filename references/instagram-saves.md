# From the client's Instagram saves (read 21 Nov 2026)

What the client keeps saving, distilled from about 2,500 saved posts across their saved collections (design 544,
fashion 658, art 497, chair 257, tattoo/piercing/hair 429, interior 34, tools 18, plus jobs and unsorted saves,
which follow the same pattern). Pet and audio saves were skipped.

How to weigh this file:
- **The client's own critique always wins.** Saves are what they look at, not what they approved on a design of
  theirs. Where a save pattern seems to contradict `principles.md` or `banned-moves.md`, the principle stands and
  the conflict is listed at the bottom.
- Use it to pick references, materials, finishes and staging before sketching, and to break ties. Each pattern
  says roughly how often it came up so the strong signals stand out.
- Borrow the hand, never the product (as in `influences.md`). Never name these makers in public copy.

## 1. The strongest signals (hundreds of saves each)

**S1. Japanese craft leather is the single biggest thread.** The two most-saved makers overall are Japanese
leather workshops (analogico, Wild Frontier Goods: about 130 saves between them, across design, fashion and art).
What they share:
- Vegetable-tanned and shell cordovan leather that ages: kakishibu (persimmon-dyed) leather, hand-painted
  cordovan, natural cowhide overlays, waxy pull-up, burnished raw edges.
- Hand saddle-stitching in a contrasting thread, visible and even. Solid brass hardware (roller and Garrison
  buckles, rivets) that is chunky and plain.
- Soft, slouchy, roomy bag shapes (shoulder bags, bucket bags, big totes, rucksacks), often with a single long
  strap, folded or wrapped closures, and colourways in tan, cognac, oxblood red, forest green and teal.
- Hand-carved, tooled and painted leather belts and wallets; sashiko and indigo on cloth.
- Odd, sculptural leather objects also recur: folded origami-style leather boxes, a black bag on carved wooden
  claw feet with horn-like handles, crumpled soft leather with crisp metal, knotted tethers (gsongim, Tsuchiya
  Kaban, Hermès runway bags).
- Use: for bags, belts and small leather, start from honest veg-tan or cordovan, visible hand stitching, solid
  brass, and a soft volume. This matches D2 (oxblood, tan) and adds forest green and teal as accepted leather
  colours.

**S2. Japanese repro denim and Americana workwear.** The biggest block of the fashion saves (about a third):
selvedge raw denim, 1940s and 50s jean and jacket repros (Blue Route, TCB, Kapital, visvim, bespoke jean makers),
chain-stitched hems, leather patches, hand repairs and sashiko patching, faded and fully worn-in indigo, horsehide
A-2 and cafe-racer leather jackets with a deep patina, engineer and work boots from Japanese bootmakers (Sagara,
John Lofgren style), hand-stitched buttonholes, factory and sewing-machine process videos.
- Use: garments and boots earn their look from honest construction, real wear and patina, and one well-chosen
  detail (a patch, a rivet, a chain-stitch), not from added ornament. Washed indigo and washed charcoal (D2) are
  confirmed.

**S3. Studio furniture craft, with carved and sculpted surfaces.** The chair, design and art collections are
dominated by the studio furniture lineage and by carved surface:
- George Nakashima (live edges, butterfly keys, the Conoid chair sketches), James Krenov (small cabinets on
  stands, figured wood, tight joinery), sculpted rocking chairs in the Maloof line (the most-saved chair type),
  Windsors and spindle-backs, Greene and Greene, Finn Juhl, Charlotte Perriand, Wharton Esherick-like arches.
- Japanese and Korean contemporary work: wiped-urushi walnut and tochi cabinets and shrines (black or clear
  urushi over grain), Korean cabinets and chairs reimagined in dark wood, small curved-marquetry boxes and
  bud vases.
- Carved surface, again and again: gouge-rippled panels ("sazanami" waves), fluted and scalloped faces,
  honeycomb-pierced wood, carved topographic tops in charred black wood, a mountain carved into a coffee table,
  stacked and layered wood strata, faceted carved vessels.
- Mixed materials as one object: wood with stone (stone blocks with wooden drawers, basalt with walnut), wood
  with cast clear aqua resin, ebonized or charred wood with butterfly keys, walnut with small brass details, a
  live-edge slab bench with a fur cushion.
- Use: this confirms the Fawn Orchard direction (gouge scoops, walnut, oiled) and A5 (sculpted surfaces). For new
  furniture, reach for one carved surface language per piece, honest joinery shown proudly (butterfly keys,
  through-tenons, dovetails), and one contrasting material treated as a peer, not a trim.

**S4. Ornamental tattoo linework, especially bone and calligraphy.** About 430 saves. The lead is the liquid
calligraphy artist already in `influences.md` (26 saves, by far the most in that collection). Around it:
symmetrical spine, rib and vertebra ornament down the back and arm, blackwork chrysanthemums with abstract
calligraphy, oriental ink sleeves, red sculpted tribal on the chest and shoulders, gothic thorned grills, corset
piercings.
- Use: for jewelry and cast ornament, symmetrical bone linework (a spine of repeated, graded vertebra-like
  elements along a central line) is a strong, on-taste structural motif. It fits A2 (motif as structure), B1
  (strokes swell and taper) and B9 (ordered rhythm in bundles).

## 2. Clear secondary signals (dozens of saves)

**S5. Jewelry and metal the client saves.**
- Organic, molten-looking cast silver with scattered small coloured stones, coral and branch-like rings, hammered
  silver tags, gothic tracery and lace necklaces with tiny bows and leaves, the feathered and thorned work already
  in `influences.md`.
- Vintage watches with a sculptural case: Cartier Tank and hidden-dial pieces, Patek Golden Ellipse, integrated
  bracelet dress watches, A. Lange, an asymmetric 1970s fashion-house watch. The common thread is an unusual
  case shape with a plain, quiet dial and a bracelet that is part of the case.
- Hand tools as jewels: infill planes in brass and exotic wood, rosewood-and-brass chisels, small spokeshaves.
  Matches the Harold and Saxon bar already listed.
- Use: for men's and machined pieces, the watch and tool saves back the "quiet, precise, one idea" direction in
  `domains.md` (machined metal). A sculpted case shape around a plain field is a good model.

**S6. Dark, avant-garde fashion.** Alongside the workwear: distressed and dipped leather boots and back-zip boots
in the Guidi and Carol Christian Poell vein, Kei Kagami's structural heels (already listed), perforated woven
leather boots, sculptural black knits with ruffles and cut-outs, laced and corseted leather, a red vinyl trench,
gothic couples in all black. Matches A7 (dark beats pretty).

**S7. Staging and photography.** The furniture and object posts the client saves are shot as product photos: a
single object on a seamless grey or white sweep, soft side light, the material reading true (grain, stitching,
patina), or in a gallery on a concrete floor. Gregory Beson (about 20 saves) is the most-saved furniture maker.
Some of his shots are on a black sweep, but D3 (white unless asked) stands.

**S8. Colour.** Across everything saved: natural tan and cognac, oxblood and bright red, black, faded indigo,
forest green and teal leather, pale natural wood against dark walnut and black urushi, aqua resin as a rare
accent. Matches D2; adds green and teal for leather and a pale-wood/dark-wood contrast for furniture.

## 3. Weaker or older threads (noted, not to lead with)
- Cute and kawaii objects (small glow figurines, character rugs, painted Yixing teapots, toy-like packaging).
  These are the older saves. They are not the design language of the client's work: A7 (dark beats pretty) wins.
- Mid-century Brazilian and Italian pieces (rosewood, velvet, chrome legs; walnut and brass cabinets) and
  Japanese timber houses and tea rooms with deep eaves, for interiors.
- 3D-printed collectible furniture and robot-like product design: an interest, not yet a direction.

## Conflicts with the client's own critique (critique wins)
- Chrome-Hearts-like silver (crosses, daggers, gothic logos) shows up in saves. C3 bans borrowed brand
  signatures ("a chrome hearts rip off isnt what i am looking for"). Take the weight and darkness, never the
  signature shapes.
- Dark red stones in gothic grills. D1 (light stones, "a lil dark for my taste") stands for the client's own
  jewelry.
- Some saved liquid-chrome calligraphy pieces are all liquid. The client said an all-liquid piece "reads as
  plumbing" and that men's pieces should not look melted; keep it as an accent.
- Black-sweep product photos. D3 white backgrounds stand.
