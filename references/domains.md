# The taste in each medium

The principles are the same everywhere; what changes is how a medium breaks them. Each section says what has
already gone wrong and what passing looks like.

## Cast metal: jewelry
- Loved: open hind wings with loose, layered veins crossing over and under inside the rim; light aquamarine heads
  on wing-frame shanks; curl-tail drops; unequal chain-and-pear drops; open vein loops inside a band.
- Missed: vein fills that are dense, combed, random or noisy (a whole stud family "a mess"); bug bodies as spiky
  clusters; nubs on every tail; dark stones; ten near-identical cuffs.
- Every face of a ring carries detail. Wearables: no sharp points, wire and links at least 1.0 mm.
- Reference for a finished piece: Bombyx SENMURV ("looks super good", 21 Nov): carved plumes from one root, a
  claw-set eye tied in by several members, every end drawn to a point or buried. Measure new pieces against it.
- Fidelity bar (21 Nov): it must pass as a phone photo of real cast sterling. Oxidised recesses with rubbed-bright
  high points, barbs and cuts deep enough to hold black, three relief depths at the focal point, a low hard light and
  a cast shadow on white. How: sculpted-metal-casting `render-fidelity.md` and `LOOK=fidelity`.
- Domain rules: sculpted-metal-casting `style-rules.md`, `client-picks.md`, `casting-rules.md`.

## Machined metal (jewelry as precision parts)
- The brief: "more masculine, maybe less gemstoned, more precise machining ... an ode to precise machining ...
  knurling elements or other like American industrial kind of things" (21 Nov).
- Every detail is a real part's real detail at its real proportion: cut knurl, involute teeth that mesh, threads
  under screw heads, springs with closed ends, chamfered edges. A detail that only looks mechanical is a fail.
- Two-tone by finish (bright, satin, oxidised black, stainless). Black parts get bright cut chamfers or they read
  as holes. Stones only where the real part has a ball.
- Piece types come from the parts, one per job, so the set reads as one machine and not as rings in a new metal.
  But every type must be something people actually wear now ("who the fuck wears bolo ties man", 21 Nov): no
  bolo ties, no novelty types picked only to avoid a repeat.
- The concept carrying the page is not enough ("i love this thinking but every piece in governor is ugly", 21 Nov).
  Round 1 failed because each piece was a literal, life-size diagram of the part: flat plates, square bars, a
  cutaway bore, plain bands, gadgets glued onto a shank. The machine is the source, not the object. Each piece has
  to be beautiful jewelry first (proportion, a flowing silhouette, volume that catches light, detail at jewelry
  scale) and read as the part second. Test: hide the story; would someone still want to wear it?
- Line (rubric 7) reads as: straight where the part is straight, true arcs, crisp edges, no wobble, no blobs.
- Round 2 failed too ("still think governor is really ugly"; "i dont like any of the pieces atm", 21 Nov): melted,
  swelling liquid lines and blobby cups are the wrong language for men's jewelry. The client's reference for men's
  pieces is a downtown men's chain-and-bar maker (URL in the critique log). What it does, and what to copy in spirit:
  - Real, plain chains as the body of the piece: cable, anchor, box, curb, snake, ball. Mostly 2 to 6 mm, worn
    loose. Mixed chains in one piece (fine beside heavy) and multi-wrap are the signature moves.
  - One "disruption" per piece: a single bar, tube, ID plate or ring breaking the chain. Never more than one idea.
  - Hand-forged and distressed: hammer marks, softened edges, an oxidised grey patina rubbed back on the high
    points. Nothing mirror-bright, nothing sculpted, nothing swollen.
  - One small signature stone (a tiny ruby), set flush or as a ball, off-centre. Never a gem cluster.
  - Small and wearable every day. Quiet. The bracelet laid in a loop on white is the hero image.
  - Test: would this sit in that maker's case without looking like a sculpture? If it looks melted, organic or
    like a model of a machine, it fails.
- So for machined pieces: the machine part is the one disruption element (a turned spool as the bar, a drilled
  plate as the ID tag, a ring gear as the key ring), carried on honest chain or a plain band, distressed, small.

## Cast metal: furniture-scale (sawhorses, case hardware, jail grilles, tools)
- Loved: Durumi's crane foot and bojagi seat, Thread's twisted-strand foot, Edge's tapered side profile, thorned
  vines up the posts, the carved-feather stretcher, the cast braided-lancet jail with the moth hanging like a bat,
  red stitched-cord leather grips ("perfect").
- Missed: smooth chrome parts on a carved piece, ball tips, literal butterflies, hexagon hubs, elf-shoe toes, flat
  laser-cut grilles, low-poly tools, metal through glass, spikes too thin to survive.
- Hardware for an existing piece starts from that piece's own motifs (list them first).
- Domain rules: sculpted-metal-casting `furniture-scale.md`, `design-coherence.md`, `buildable.md`.

## Cast seating (chairs, 23 Nov)
- Rejected: the ten cast PU chairs (spine, cathedral window, claw ribs, spiked halo, organ pipes, leaf, thorns,
  coil, plates, sinews): "ugly or campy or kitsche". Each depicted a thing; glossy black foam on splayed tube legs
  read as a Halloween prop.
- Direction: A13 and the Sachs / Gehry / Rams entry in influences.md. One cast mass, one idea from the process,
  the fewest other parts (stock plate cut and bent by file, stock bolts left visible), mood from proportion,
  mass and pigment.

## Cut sheet, stacked (SendCutSend laminations: Nave, Ogive, Crocket)
- Depth comes from stacking edge-on profiles, but the layer ends are where it fails. Fixed inward offsets of one
  outline leave each outer layer ending in a square step, which reads as a stub (blind critique of Crocket r1,
  22 Nov: "square, stepped layer ends ... read as unfinished cuts"). Build every layer from the same strokes at
  reduced width, and make each outer pair stop a little earlier at every tip in a long taper, so the ends nest
  as points and the centre layer runs on alone to the point.
- A wall plate or base under a laminated stroke hugs it: a narrow stepped plate a few mm wider than the stack,
  not a wide blank panel with the piece laid on it.
- A spur or brace lands on the plate or the next member; a thin point ending in air beside a screw reads as an
  afterthought.
- Clear and metal laminations (Nave v3, 23 Nov): put structure in metal (floors, magnet and hardware layers, ribs,
  handles) and the decorative layers in clear acrylic, so the construction (magnets, pins, the bands) shows through.
  Bands of metal that line up across pieces (a deep bin's bands level with stacked trays beside it) read as one system.
  Nothing hidden inside a clear wall: lightening pockets and glue lines show.
- A repeated hollow in a grid plate keeps one orientation. Vesicas alternating ±45° cell to cell read as
  flowers, and spurs off the grid ribs read as barbs (Nave Plinth rounds, 23 Nov). Let the ribs and bosses carry it.

## Wood furniture
- Fawn Orchard (the client's own carving: gouge scoops along the grain, pierced lancets) is that piece's language,
  not a default. A sweeping laminated form is faired smooth, one wood, one finish (Shrine bench, 24 Nov: "clean
  sweep no holes or carving marks", "smooth it all out like u would with an angle grinder", "all walnut with tung
  oil", "no two tone"). When asked for "only solid wood carving", every metal, ply or bought part goes (keep what the client
  names, like upholstered seats).
- A laminated piece looks laminated: every layer and stave its own board, figure breaking at the glue lines,
  layer rings as contour bands where the surface is ground through the stack ("the grain wouldnt look like that
  because they are multiple pieces laminated not a single board being bent", Shrine bench, 24 Nov). One tone still.
  Everywhere it is shown: the site's 3D viewer too, not only the renders ("grain is wrong again", 25 Nov: the viewer
  wrapped one flat texture round the twist). Wood is a solid; no texture may follow a curve like a bent board.
- Upholstery follows the wood's curve, plush with soft rolled edges (dense short-pile mohair/velvet refs), in the
  colours the client set: Shrine bench seats stay one mint, one pink ("i still want one pink and one kinda mint seat").
- Match photos one to one when modelling existing work; place every part exactly where the photo shows it.
- Render on the studio theme: white seamless studio, soft high light, a real contact shadow (D3). Not staged rooms,
  curtains or rugs ("the site product photography should be on theme not whatever these are", 24 Nov).
- Domain rules: hardwood-furniture-cad, seating-load-check.

## Leather: boots and bags
Round 1 "all suck and feel unrealised", round 2 "super ugly and unoriginal", round 3 "simple ... details are
inconsistent in quality ... a lot of flat surfaces ... flimsy or floppy ... gimmicky". Only COAT, HALO and
GAUNTLET had ideas worth keeping. What round 3 looked like: nine near-identical plain black calf shells with the
same long point and the same block heel, each carrying one small attachment (stock rectangle buckles, a ring on
the ankle, a thin bracket heel, stitch-line vines, single-layer crown points).

What passing needs:
- **The idea in the structure.** The heel, counter, shaft or toe *is* the motif, built as a sculpted part that
  carries load (a cast heel cage that is the halo; lames that are the shaft; a horn that is the heel and counter
  sweeping up the back, not a ring on the side).
- **No plain shell.** Every boot gets modelled leather over most of its surface: layered and lapped panels with
  real edge thickness, moulded or wet-formed shapes, crimped fronts, channel quilting or couched cord in relief,
  boxed and piped edges, overlays with turned edges. A single smooth calf vamp is a flat surface.
- **Different silhouettes.** Nine boots share no default last, toe or heel unless the brief asks.
- **Hardware in the piece's language,** cast and big enough to read at sheet size, integrated with the leather
  (straps run through cast keepers that are part of the motif). Rectangle stock buckles fail.
- **Stiff where it stands up.** Points, flaps and crowns are double layers with turned or bound edges, stiffened
  (leatherboard, moulding) or carried by metal. Nothing single-layer sticks up or hangs.
- **Even fidelity.** If the hardware is jewelry-grade, the welt, sole edge, heel stack and topline are too.
- Domain rules: boots-and-bags `check-loop.md`, `construction.md`, `hardware.md`.

## Leather: jackets and garments
- Same critique as the boots (rejected together). Round 3 jackets read as glossy grey plastic with noisy
  wrinkles, one rich detail (sleeve lames, a collar) on an otherwise blank body, and thin skirts and tails that
  hang like sheet.
- Passing: matte leather that reads as hide; the motif carried by the cut and the seams across the whole garment
  (not one zone); structured parts (collars, peplums, tails, wings) built as stiffened double layers with finished
  edges; hardware cast in the motif and sized to read at full-garment view; each jacket the boot's twin at a
  glance.
- Renders (the client, 25 Nov): always on a plain white faceless figure (CLO's mannequin, or the avatar painted flat
  white with no hair, briefs or shoes). Never a realistic person.

- Shop uniform (the client, 25-26 Nov, rounds 5-9): the liked baseline is the round-5 two-tone set (Soft Condor peach
  and ash, straight six-tail dovetail yoke, boxy cropped jacket, wide calf-length skirt-shorts); iterate small from it.
  A dovetail joint is the actual cut edge where two leather panels meet (tails cut into one panel, pins into the
  other, sharp laser corners, flared tails), never tail shapes applied on top or rounded "castle" fingers. A trade
  uniform earns its identity from storage for that trade's real tools (two Bridge City combination squares, a 6 in
  flat rule, Blackwing pencils, a plane in the trouser bag), and every pocket is shown holding its real tool;
  an empty pocket strip reads as a mess. Hidden hardware (magnets, stiffeners) shows only as a soft dome and a
  stitched ring; a disc or button sitting on the surface is a fake.

## Machines (Hivemind, formerly the Dancers)
- Never frame machines or robots as people serving someone, and never as women: a machine is "it", not "she" or "her".
  The group was renamed from The Dancers on 25 Nov 2026 because the theme read as "a bunch of subservient robots
  being women" (the client). the client kept Queen Bee; the other machines got bee and hive names with no gender (no "worker" or "drone"). Renamed group: Hivemind.
- Real parts only, matched to their listings; no stand-in boxes. Saturated colours must survive the renderer.
- Look is engineered and honest; the taste rules on coherence and fidelity still apply (one part rendered
  rougher than the rest stands out).

## Site pages and renders
- Plan the frames before rendering: hero, bleed-crop details, ortho pair, scale, process, family (`productdesign-account.md` section 10).
- White backgrounds by default (D3): every render, page image, teaser and part photo. Check the actual output
  file, not the render: a transparent PNG flattened with a plain RGB convert lands on black.
- Every page matches the newest page look (currently Nine Rests): same layout, type and theme. A page that passes
  the brand audit can still look old.
- Every image, GLB and spec shows the newest model version. Caching or a stale file reads as "not done".
- 3D viewers: no flicker, no shattered decimation, grain running along each part, stones in their colours. Wood textures in a viewer are straight grain only, with real end grain on the ends; never a flat-sawn, cathedral or swirl image (the client, 23 Nov: "how did this grain get fucked again").
- Interactive pieces feel like product pages: images act as controls where they look like buttons.
- Reels: the preferred format is reference to result (influences, Presentation and grade): references stack
  over the piece, peel away, and the finished piece holds clean. Lamps are shot turning in the dark.
- Fractal and digital presets match their named artist until the render could pass as theirs.
